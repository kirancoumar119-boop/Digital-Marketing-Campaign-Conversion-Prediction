Problem & goals

What the project set out to solve

Problem statement

  • Marketing spend is wasted when every lead is treated the same, regardless of conversion likelihood.
  • True converters are rare — under 12% of training rows — so naive models default to predicting "no conversion."
  • A 0.50 decision cutoff ignores the real asymmetry between order value and acquisition cost.
  • The goal is a model and a threshold that jointly maximize net profit, not just accuracy.

Project objectives

  • Engineer behavioral features that capture engagement, not just raw counts.
  • Prevent data leakage at every preprocessing step.
  • Benchmark classical, ensemble, and boosting models under identical conditions.
  • Tune the strongest candidates and extract the real conversion drivers.
  • Translate the model's output into a dollar-denominated spending rule.

Feature engineering

Four ratios that describe intent, not just activity

Raw counts like "5 email opens" say little on their own. Dividing related metrics against each other surfaces efficiency and intensity signals the raw columns miss.

CTOR — Click-to-Open Rate
EmailClicks / (EmailOpens + ε)

How often an opened email actually gets clicked.

Engagement Intensity
PagesPerVisit × TimeOnSite

Combines depth and duration into one engagement score.

Spend per Visit
AdSpend / (WebsiteVisits + 1)

Cost efficiency of the traffic a campaign bought.

Loyalty Velocity
LoyaltyPoints / (PreviousPurchases + 1)

Reward accumulation relative to purchase history.

Preprocessing

Every transformation is fit on the training split alone

Outlier bounds, VIF reduction, scaling, and SMOTE are all computed from the 6,400-row training partition, then applied unchanged to the 1,600-row holdout — the fix for a leaky pipeline.

Class balance before & after SMOTE

6,400-row training split

Natural conversions sit at roughly 7.1 converted customers for every 1 non-converter. SMOTE synthesizes minority-class rows in the transformed training set only, balancing it to 5,610 / 5,610 before any model sees it.

Business impact

The 0.50 cutoff was leaving money on the table

Using an assumed Average Order Value of $250 and a marketing cost of $45 per contacted lead, net profit was simulated across probability thresholds from 0.10 to 0.90 on the holdout set.

Lowering the cutoff from 0.50 to 0.40 catches a few more true converters than it adds in wasted spend on false positives, given this $250 / $45 value ratio — a ~0.4% profit lift from a one-line change to the decision rule, with no retraining required.

Key findings

What the model means for marketing strategy

Four findings tie the feature importance, channel behavior, engagement signal, and profit curve back to a concrete marketing decision.

01
Focus on buyers, not just browsers
What we found

Generic brand-awareness campaigns didn't move the needle. Campaigns built specifically for conversion were the single biggest driver in the model, accounting for over 20% of its overall decision-making power.

The takeaway

It's easy to burn budget chasing attention. Shift spend away from general visibility and into high-intent conversion campaigns.

02
Quality over quantity with channels
What we found

Not all channels are equal. Paid Search (PPC) and Referral consistently brought in higher-quality leads than broad social media clicks.

The takeaway

Spreading spend thin across every platform underperforms. Concentrate budget on channels like PPC, where people are already looking to buy.

03
How people interact matters more than how often
What we found

Raw counts — email clicks, page visits — didn't tell the full story. What mattered was composite engagement: how long someone stayed combined with how many pages they viewed.

The takeaway

Traffic is vanity, engagement is sanity. Score and target leads by depth of interaction, not visit count alone.

04
Smarter budgeting with profit curves
What we found

A standard 50% cutoff is a modeling default, not a business number. Mapping cost ($45 CAC) against value ($250 AOV) across thresholds showed that a 40% cutoff actually nets more profit.

The takeaway

Default settings leave money on the table. Tune the decision threshold to real business economics to capture more good leads without overspending on bad ones.

Deployment

A live scoring tool, not just a notebook

The tuned pipeline is serialized and served through a Streamlit app, so a marketer can score a new lead without touching Python.

What the app does

  • Collects demographic, campaign, and behavioral inputs through form controls.
  • Recomputes the four engineered ratios (CTOR, Engagement Intensity, Spend per Visit, Loyalty Velocity) live.
  • Runs the saved ColumnTransformer and tuned XGBoost model via joblib.
  • Returns a conversion probability and a high / low confidence call in real time.

Conclusion

What this project actually shows

  • Campaign type and channel dominate — conversion-focused campaigns and PPC/referral channels carry the strongest signal, well ahead of any single behavioral metric.
  • Boosting beat bagging — XGBoost and Gradient Boosting consistently outperformed Random Forest and the stacking ensemble on this feature set.
  • Leakage discipline mattered more than model choice — fitting every transform on the training split alone is what makes the 91.6% holdout accuracy trustworthy.
  • Recall over precision, by design — at 99% recall, the model is tuned to miss almost no real converter, accepting some false positives in return.
  • The threshold is a business decision, not a default — re-pricing the cutoff against AOV and CAC recovered profit without touching the model itself.
  • Next step: pair this classifier with a customer lifetime value regression, so ad spend is capped by predicted value, not just predicted probability.
